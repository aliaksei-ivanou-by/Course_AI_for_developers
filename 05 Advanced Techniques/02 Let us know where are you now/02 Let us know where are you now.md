# Where Are You Now in AI-Assisted Development?

![AI coding iceberg showing progressively more advanced tools and agent workflows](AI%20Coding%20Iceberg.png)

This short lesson is a reflection checkpoint before the advanced material. Describe how you currently use AI in software development so that your starting point, practical experience, and next learning goals are explicit.

The illustration presents AI-development practices as a humorous iceberg. Treat its labels as conversation prompts, not as an objective maturity model or a ranking of developers. Effective practice depends on whether a workflow improves real engineering outcomes, not on how many tools or agents it uses.

## Reflection Prompt

In free-text form, describe your current level of AI usage in software development.

You may use the following questions to structure your response:

- Which AI assistants, coding agents, or development platforms do you use?
- How often do you use them, and for which engineering tasks?
- Do you use project instructions, structured specifications, skills, MCP servers, subagents, or agent teams?
- How do you review generated code and verify correctness, security, and maintainability?
- Have you worked with open-source models or open-source agent harnesses?
- What limitations currently slow you down or prevent wider adoption?
- Which workflow or capability would you most like to improve next?

Concrete examples are more useful than a list of product names. Mention what you asked the AI to do, how much supervision it required, how you validated the result, and what outcome you achieved.

## Why This Checkpoint Matters

An honest baseline helps you choose advanced topics that address a real gap. It also gives you a reference point for measuring progress later. The goal is not to reach the bottom of the iceberg; it is to build a reliable, proportionate process for the software you develop.

## Key Takeaway

Describe your present workflow in terms of tasks, tools, supervision, validation, and outcomes. Use that baseline to select the next advanced practice worth learning rather than adopting complexity for its own sake.
